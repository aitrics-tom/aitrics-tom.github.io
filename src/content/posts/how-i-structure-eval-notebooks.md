## The notebook has a job

An evaluation notebook should help someone recover the shape of an experiment quickly. That means I optimize for:

- clear framing at the top,
- visible assumptions,
- a stable section order,
- and a final recommendation block.

## My default structure

I like a repeatable sequence:

1. What question are we trying to answer?
2. What are the test slices?
3. What counts as failure?
4. What did we actually observe?
5. What decision follows from this?

## The hidden benefit

A good notebook is also a collaboration surface.

When product, engineering, and research all need to align, a durable notebook often does more work than a polished slide because it preserves the reasoning that produced the conclusion.
